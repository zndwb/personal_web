Hi, I'm Haoxin.

I'm a Master of Computer Science student at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. Most of my time goes into one question: what do large language models actually remember, and can we make them truly forget?

Removing knowledge from a model turns out to be surprisingly shallow. It often looks gone, then comes back after a little fine-tuning. Working with Prof. Ren Wang at Illinois Tech, I've been studying this in Mixture-of-Experts models and, more recently, in LLM agents that keep their own memories. I care about this because models that can't forget are hard to trust.

I didn't start out in language models. I did my undergrad at China Agricultural University in Beijing, where my first research was spotting tomato leaf disease on a phone and teaching an RL agent to tune models for tractor and harvester data. Along the way I also spent time in industry, training a diffusion model on DNA sequencing signals and building backend services around LLMs. I like work where a clean idea has to survive contact with messy, real data.

Right now

  • ResearchMaking unlearning in MoE models stick: erasing knowledge layer by layer instead of from a single expert.
  • Class projectBuilding an English–Mandarin benchmark that asks whether LLMs can tell “he was writing a letter” from “he wrote a letter.”
  • LearningWeb programming, NLP, and applied ML at UIUC, which is also why this site exists.
